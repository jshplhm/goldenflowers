for our Google & AI search

An honest, plain-English look at what changes when goldenflorals.com moves off Squarespace onto our new site — and everything we've done to protect (and improve) the ranking we've worked for.

Improved Held even Worth watching

The single most important thing

This is not a move to a new address. The website stays at goldenflorals.com — the exact same domain Google already knows and trusts. We're only swapping what's behind that address (Squarespace → our new site).

That distinction is everything. Google treats this as a site update, not a relocation, so there's no "change of address" penalty and no reset of the trust the domain has built. On top of that, every existing web address either still works or automatically forwards to the right new page — we verified all 60 of them, zero broken links. The two things that most often tank SEO during a redesign (a new domain, and broken links) simply don't apply here.

Below, topic by topic, is where each site is genuinely stronger, our verdict, and exactly what we did or checked. We've kept it honest — including the couple of places the old site still holds an edge.

01Web addresses & broken links

The #1 way a website move damages SEO is old links that suddenly go nowhere (404 errors). Google drops those pages and the ranking they carried.

Every old link preserved or redirected Improved

Squarespace (current)

Owns all the current addresses natively — nothing to redirect while it's live.

New site

Keeps the exact same blog addresses, and forwards every older address to its new home.

What we did & verified
Pulled the live Squarespace sitemap (60 addresses), matched every one against the new site, and found 12 old portfolio-gallery links that would have broken — then added forwards for all 12. Re-checked: 60 of 60 covered, 0 broken. All 35 blog articles keep their identical addresses, so their ranking history carries straight over.

Being honest: the risks, and how we've limited them

  • A short settling-in period is normal. After any big site change, Google re-crawls and rankings can wobble slightly for a few days to a few weeks. This is expected and temporary — we've minimized it by keeping the same domain, addresses, and content, and we'll resubmit the sitemap the moment we switch so Google re-reads everything fast.
  • The current site has "incumbency." Squarespace's version is the one Google has indexed and trusted for a while. The new one has to be re-crawled and re-earn that familiarity. Preserving URLs + content is exactly what makes that transfer smooth rather than a restart.
  • Forwarding is slightly gentler than a hard redirect. Our host (GitHub Pages) uses a lightweight forward rather than a server-level 301. Google honors it and it's paired with the correct "this is the real page" signal, so the effect is effectively the same — worth knowing, not worth worrying about.
  • Responsive image sizes — a next step. Squarespace auto-serves different image sizes per device; the new site serves one good size. Not a problem, but it's our top follow-up to make mobile loading even faster.

What happens at launch

  • Point goldenflorals.com at the new site (the DNS switch).
  • Flip one setting so every page officially "belongs" to goldenflorals.com.
  • Resubmit the sitemap in Google Search Console and ask Google to re-read the key pages.
  • Watch Search Console for the first month — it emails us if any link ever breaks, so nothing slips through unnoticed.

The bottom line

This move is low-risk and set up to come out ahead — not behind.

Because we're keeping the same domain, the same web addresses, and the same content, the foundation of your search ranking carries over intact. Everywhere the two sites differ, the new one is equal or stronger — and in the one area that matters most for being found and recommended by Google and AI (structured data), it goes from nothing to a complete, verified setup.

Expect a brief, normal settling period right after launch, then a site that's faster, better understood by search engines, and easier to grow from here.
