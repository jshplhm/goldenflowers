<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Your flowers set the tone for everything. Here's what actually matters when vetting a florist for your mountain wedding — and the questions that reveal whether they're truly the right fit.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">You've found the venue. You've booked the photographer. Now comes choosing the florist who will translate your vision into something real, living, and unforgettable.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">In the Lake Tahoe region, you have no shortage of florists to consider. But choosing the right one is more than liking their Instagram feed. It's about trust, alignment, and knowing what questions to ask before you sign anything.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>1. Look at their work — but look deeper</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Portfolios tell you what a florist can do. But the more important question is: does their aesthetic sensibility feel like yours? Scan their gallery not just for pretty arrangements, but for a consistent point of view — a signature way they use color, texture, and movement.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">For outdoor mountain weddings, you also want to see that they understand the Sierra Nevada environment: locally sourced stems, textures that read well against granite backdrops and pine forests, designs that don't wilt in July heat or blow apart in afternoon wind.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong><em>What to ask:</em></strong><em> "Can you show me full galleries from complete weddings — not just hero shots?" Seeing the whole picture reveals how they handle logistics, scale, and cohesion.</em></p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>2. Ask about sourcing — it matters more than you think</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Where your flowers come from affects how they look, how long they last, and the kind of story you're telling with your wedding. Florists who prioritize locally grown or California-grown stems often produce arrangements with an earthier, more organic feel — and a smaller environmental footprint.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">At Golden Flowers, we're committed to sourcing locally and seasonally whenever possible. It shapes our entire design process — from the blooms we recommend to the way we build out your palette around what's actually in season on your wedding day.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>3. Understand what's actually included</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Florists vary widely in what their proposals cover. Some include delivery, setup, and breakdown; others charge separately. Some offer day-of installation management; others drop and go. Make sure you know exactly what you're getting before comparing quotes.</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Delivery, setup, and breakdown</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Ceremony arch or altar installation</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Reception table florals and centerpieces</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Personal flowers (bouquets, boutonnieres, corsages)</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Day-of florist coordination and on-site time</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Vessel and rental fees</p></li></ul><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong><em>Pro tip:</em></strong><em> Ask for an itemized proposal so you can see exactly where your budget is going. A florist who can't break it down isn't someone you want running your wedding day.</em></p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>4. Chemistry matters as much as credentials</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Your florist will be one of the last vendors you see before you walk down the aisle. You need to trust them completely. During your initial consultation, pay attention to how they listen. Do they ask questions about your vision, or do they immediately pitch their own ideas? Do they feel like a collaborator, or a vendor just processing an order?</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">The best florist relationships feel like a creative partnership. You bring the vision, the mood board, the story and they bring the botanical knowledge, the logistics, and the execution.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>5. Understand minimums and availability</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Many boutique wedding florists in the Tahoe area work with a minimum floral investment, this ensures they can give your wedding the full attention it deserves without overextending across multiple bookings. Ask upfront whether your budget aligns with their minimum, and confirm they're available for your date before investing too much time in the conversation. Our booking for a full service wedding is $5,000</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>6. Read the contract carefully</strong></h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Every reputable florist will have a contract. Before signing, confirm it covers substitution policies (what happens if a specific bloom isn't available), payment schedule and cancellation terms, and any ADA or venue-specific restrictions that might affect installation.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">If a florist doesn't offer a contract, that's a red flag, not a sign of flexibility.</p></div>
