<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">There is a particular morning, you know the one, when the light shifts, the air smells like wet earth and pine, and everything feels like it's exhaling after holding its breath all winter. That morning arrived for me this week. Spring is here, and I am completely undone by it.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">As a Lake Tahoe wedding florist, I spend most of February resting and getting excited about spring, checking in with my growers, sketching out color palettes, texting farmers I've been working with for years just to ask how their spring flowers are coming along. There's a particular kind of professional hopefulness that comes with running a floral studio in the Sierra Nevada foothills. You know the beauty is coming. You're just waiting for it to show up.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">And it has shown up. Magnificently.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><em>The meadows around Tahoe don't just bloom, they ignite. And I get to carry that into every wedding I touch.</em></p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;">What Spring Looks Like in the Sierra</h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">People sometimes ask me why I'm so committed to the Lake Tahoe region as a wedding florist when I could go anywhere. The answer is spring mornings like this one. The air at elevation has this crystalline quality, sharp and clean and impossibly alive, and when the first wildflowers start threading through the meadow grasses at lower elevations, and snow still is on the ground at higher elevations. It feels genuinely sacred.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Right now the mule's ear is sending up its first silver-green leaves along the hillsides. The lupine is weeks away but you can feel it coming. My favorite local growers are cutting the first ranunculus and anemones of the season, and I have been borderline manic texting them for updates (they are very patient with me).</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">For Lake Tahoe wedding flowers, spring is genuinely the most exciting season. Couples who book late spring and early summer dates get this extraordinary range to work with, garden roses just arriving, local peonies on their way, all of it layered against the backdrop of snowmelt and pine. It's not a palette you could manufacture. It exists here, in this specific place, for a few very fleeting weeks.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Spring Florals I'm Obsessed With Right Now</h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I've been putting together mood boards for our summer brides this week, and the themes keep pulling in the same direction: soft and wild rooted in the landscape, or editorial and edgy to make a statement. Here's what's inspiring me most right now for Tahoe weddings</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Garden ranunculus</strong></p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">These look like someone painted them by hand, layer after paper-thin layer. They're grown locally by the time late spring arrives and they photograph beautifully in the Sierra light, which is golden and diffused in a way that makes blooms glow.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Textural greens: ferns, maidenhair, and local grasses.</strong> One of my deepest convictions as a florist is that the greenery IS the design. Especially in a place like Tahoe, where the landscape is already doing so much of the work, I want the arrangements to feel continuous with the forest. Not placed against it, born from it.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Clematis vine and sweet peas for cascading installations.</strong> I've been dreaming about an arch installation that uses clematis and sweet peas trellised over a natural wood frame. For a lakeside or mountain meadow ceremony, there is nothing more breathtaking. The movement, the delicacy, it just works with Tahoe in a way that more structured florals simply don't.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;">A Note to Couples Planning a Lake Tahoe Wedding</h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">If you're in the middle of planning and you've been putting off the florist conversation, spring is the moment to have it. Because this is the season when everything feels possible. When I sit down with couples in spring, the ideas flow differently. There's something about the energy of the season that opens up the creative conversation.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I work with couples across the Lake Tahoe region, lakeside venues, meadow ceremonies, historic estates tucked into the pines, and what I love most is designing Lake Tahoe wedding flowers that feel specific to the place. Not a style you could drop into a ballroom in any city. Florals that say: <em>this happened here, in these mountains, on this particular day.</em></p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">That's what spring does to me every year. It reminds me why I chose this work and this place. The flowers know something we're still figuring out, that beauty is generous, that it comes back, that the right conditions make everything possible.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">I can't wait to see what this season brings.</p><h3 style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Planning a Tahoe wedding this summer?</h3><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Let's talk about what's in bloom and what we can build together.</p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>INQUIRE WITH GOLDEN FLOWERS, EMAIL BRITTANY@GOLDENFLORALS.COM</strong></p><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">LAKE TAHOE WEDDING FLORIST | LAKE TAHOE WEDDING FLOWERS | SPRING FLORALS | SIERRA NEVADA WEDDINGS | LOCALLY GROWN FLOWERS | SUSTAINABLE FLORISTRY | TAHOE WEDDING INSPIRATION</p><p class="" data-rte-preserve-empty="true" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"></p></div>
