<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">There is a moment every summer, sometime in late July, when I walk out to the dahlia rows on our farm plots and just stop. The plants are taller than I expected again. The first blooms are opening. And I remember, the way I do every year, why I fell so completely in love with this flower.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">My grandmother grew them. My dad grew them. I grew up around their dinner-plate faces and their impossible color range and their stubborn, generous way of producing more blooms the more you cut them. When I started Golden Flowers, there was never a question about what we would grow. Dahlias were always going to be a crop we grew.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Why Dahlias Are the Perfect Wedding Flower</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">If you've ever seen a dahlia up close, really up close, the way you do when you're cutting them at six in the morning with dew still on the petals, you understand why they photograph so beautifully. The layers of petals catch light differently at every angle. The colors are saturated in a way that feels almost unreal. And the variety is staggering: café au lait dahlias with their muted, dusty warmth. Deep burgundy ball dahlias that anchor a bouquet like nothing else. White dinner plates so large they're practically a centerpiece on their own.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">For wedding florals, dahlias offer something that imported flowers often can't: genuine presence. They fill space without feeling heavy. They hold up through a full wedding day. And because we grow them ourselves, we can cut them at exactly the right stage: not too tight, not too open, for your specific event date.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What Happens on the Farm</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Our dahlia operation runs across three growing plots at our farm. We start with tubers, this year we have over 1,000, which go into the ground around Mother’s Day in our growing zone. For the first several weeks, there's nothing to see. Dahlias are slow starters. They put their energy into root development before they show you anything above the soil.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">By early summer the plants are climbing. We use stakes and horizontal netting to support them as they grow, dahlias can reach five or six feet and need structure to keep from toppling in wind or rain. We pinch the early growth to encourage branching, which means more stems and more blooms per plant rather than one tall, single-stemmed flower.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Then comes the waiting. And then, almost all at once, the farm transforms.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Cutting and Conditioning</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Harvesting dahlias for weddings is a precise process. We cut in the early morning, when temperatures are cool and the stems are fully hydrated from overnight. Each stem gets cut at an angle and goes immediately into buckets of water. From there, the flowers move into our cooler for a conditioning period, usually 12 to 24 hours, which allows them to fully hydrate and firm up before they're used in arrangements.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">This step is what separates farm-fresh dahlias from flowers that have been in transit for days. A properly conditioned, locally grown dahlia will look as good at the end of your wedding reception as it did when you walked down the aisle.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Dahlias and the Tahoe Wedding Season</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">One of the reasons dahlias work so well for Lake Tahoe weddings is timing. Peak dahlia season in Northern California runs from mid-July through October, which maps almost perfectly onto the busiest stretch of the Tahoe wedding calendar. Late summer and early fall brides have access to the fullest, most abundant dahlia harvest of the year.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">For couples marrying outside that window, we work with trusted regional growers and select imported varieties that meet our quality standards. But if your date falls between July and October, there is nothing quite like having flowers that were growing in the ground a week before your wedding.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What Dahlias Look Like in Wedding Florals</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">We use dahlias across almost every element of a wedding design, bridal and bridesmaid bouquets, boutonnieres, ceremony arches, reception centerpieces, and large-scale installations. Their versatility is part of what makes them so valuable as a design flower.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">In a bouquet, a few large dinner plate dahlias paired with smaller ball varieties and loose garden foliage create that lush, gathered-from-the-garden look that photographs beautifully in Tahoe's natural light. In a ceremony arch, dahlias provide the visual weight and color saturation that makes the structure read across a large outdoor space. In a bud vase or small reception arrangement, a single stem is enough.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">There is almost no floral context where dahlias don't belong. Which, if you ask me, is exactly as it should be.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">A Flower Worth Growing</h3><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">I think often about my grandmother's garden and the rows of dahlias she tended every summer with the same quiet attention she gave everything she loved. My dad carries that same relationship with them. And somewhere along the way, so do I.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Growing dahlias for wedding couples feels like a continuation of something that was handed down to me, a belief that beautiful flowers, grown with care, have a way of making people feel something on the most important days of their lives.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">That's why we farm. And that's why dahlias will always be at the heart of what we do at Golden Flowers.</p><p class="font-claude-response-body break-words whitespace-normal leading-[1.7]" style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><em>Golden Flowers is a farm-connected, foam-free wedding floral studio based in Incline Village, Nevada. Our dahlias are grown on our farm and harvested fresh for each wedding we design. We'd love to be part of yours.</em></p></div>
