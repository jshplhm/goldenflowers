<div class="sqs-html-content" data-sqsp-text-block-content><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">When you start planning your wedding flowers, you probably aren't thinking about floral foam. But if you've found yourself drawn to florists who describe their work as "foam-free" or "sustainable," you may be wondering what that actually means, and whether it changes anything about how your flowers look on your wedding day.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">The short answer is it changes quite a bit. Here's what you need to know.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What Is Floral Foam, Exactly?</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Floral foam, most commonly sold under the brand name Oasis, is the dense green brick you've likely seen at the bottom of grocery store arrangements. It holds water and keeps stems in place, which made it a staple of the floral industry for decades.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">The problem is that floral foam is a single-use plastic. It doesn't biodegrade. It breaks down into microplastics that end up in waterways and soil. And here in the Lake Tahoe region, where we are surrounded by some of the most pristine wilderness in the country, that's not something we're willing to contribute to.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What Do Foam-Free Florists Use Instead?</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Foam-free design isn't a compromise, it's a craft. At Golden Flowers, we use a combination of techniques and materials that have actually been used by florists for generations, before foam became the easy default:</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><strong>Chicken wire </strong> anchor stems beautifully in vessels and vases, giving arrangements that lush, spilling quality that foam-designed pieces often can't achieve.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><strong>Grid taping</strong> making a grid out of tape for table flowers is a great substitute for floral foam. </p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><strong>Flower frogs</strong>, the heavy ceramic or metal inserts that sit at the base of a vessel. They are endlessly reusable and hold arrangements with remarkable stability.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><strong>Hand-tied technique</strong> is used for bouquets and many installation pieces, which means the flowers are simply arranged, tied, and trimmed, no synthetic material required.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><strong>Water tubes</strong> keep individual stems hydrated in larger installation work, like arches and ceremony columns.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">The result? Arrangements that look more natural, more alive, and more at home in the Sierra Nevada landscape we design within.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Does Foam-Free Mean My Flowers Won't Last?</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">This is the most common concern we hear, and it's completely understandable. The answer is no, your flowers will be just as fresh and beautiful as any traditionally designed arrangement, as long as your florist knows what they're doing.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">In fact, many florists who have made the switch to foam-free find that their flowers actually last longer. Floral foam, once it dries out, cannot rehydrate properly, meaning stems that lose their water source mid-event are in trouble. With foam-free mechanics, stems remain in direct contact with water throughout the day.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What Does This Mean for the Look of My Wedding Flowers?</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Foam-free florals tend to have a more garden-gathered, naturalistic quality. Stems move more freely. Blooms face different directions. Foliage drapes and trails the way it would in nature. If you've been saving images of lush, romantic, the kind that look like they were cut from a garden that morning. That aesthetic is actually much easier to achieve without foam.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">For couples marrying at Tahoe's mountain venues, meadow elopements, or lakeside ceremonies, this approach feels completely at home in the landscape. The flowers become part of the environment rather than sitting apart from it.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Why We are making the Choice to Go Foam-Free</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">At Golden Flowers, our commitment to foam-free design comes from the same place as everything else we do. We farm our own cut flowers and support local farmers, which means we have a direct relationship with how flowers are grown, harvested, and handled. Introducing synthetic, non-biodegradable materials at the end of that process would feel completely at odds with the care that goes into everything before it. We do use foam when the design absolutely requires it, but we are actively striving to decrease our use of foam even further. We are on a mission to become 100% foam free by the end of this year. It is important to note that the rare times in which we do use foam, we use the eco-lines or agra-wool foam that is reusable and decomposes. </p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">We also believe that the most beautiful wedding flowers are the ones that reflect the place where you're getting married. In the Sierra Nevada, that means working with nature, not against it.</p><h3 style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Questions to Ask Your Florist About Sustainability</h3><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">If sustainability matters to you as you're interviewing florists, here are a few questions worth asking:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Do you use floral foam in your designs?</p></li><li><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">How do you hydrate stems in centerpieces and installations?</p></li><li><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">Do you source any flowers locally or regionally?</p></li><li><p style="white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">What happens to the flowers after the wedding?</p></li></ul><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true">A florist who has thought carefully about these questions will have clear, confident answers. One who hasn't may be worth a follow-up conversation.</p><p style="text-align: start; ; white-space:pre-wrap;" data-rte-preserve-empty="true"><em>Golden Flowers is on the path to be completely foam-free, and is a sustainably sourced wedding floral studio based in Incline Village, Nevada, serving the Lake Tahoe and Sierra Nevada region. Our flowers are grown on our farm, and sourced from trusted regional growers. We'd love to design your wedding day.</em></p></div>
